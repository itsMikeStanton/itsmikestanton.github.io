    Our Recommendation

    The site does not lack features. It has fifteen years of additions and no history of editing down — two of every five pages belong to an audio tour that moved elsewhere, and roughly two thirds have not been touched since 2020. A rebuild that faithfully ports everything moves the debt and charges for the move. The value here is deciding what the site is for.

    01

    What you’re Rebuilding

    walkway.org is not a park-information site with a few extras bolted on. It is running ten separate jobs at once — a store, a membership program, a donation platform, an events engine, a volunteer pipeline, a press archive, and a full self-guided audio tour of the Hudson Valley. A rebuild that treats it as a brochure will drop most of that.

    Two facts shape the whole project. First, the site’s money and data mostly leave the site. Donations and membership run through Neon CRM, ticketing through GiveSmart, payments through Square, email through two different platforms, volunteering through a fourth system. Much of what walkway.org “does” is hand people to somewhere else, and those handoffs are the things that break quietly in a migration.

    Second, a large part of the site is no longer maintained. Roughly two of every five pages belong to an audio tour last touched in 2017 — a tour that has since moved to a different platform entirely. Deciding what happens to that content is the single biggest scoping question in front of you.

      How this was put together

      The inventory comes from the site's own WordPress REST API, which is publicly readable, cross-checked against its four sitemaps. A sampled crawl then recorded where pages link out to, what forms they carry, and what they embed. Counts are of published content objects; the media library is counted separately.

      What this phase could not see

      • No traffic or revenue data. The site still runs the retired Universal Analytics tag and has no GA4 property, so there is no behavioral record to draw on. Everything here describes what exists, not what gets used.
      • No admin access. Drafts, redirects, user roles, and form destinations are not visible from outside — we can see a contact form, not where it emails.
      • Purposes are inferred from URL structure, titles, templates, and outbound links — not confirmed with staff.
      • Events were sampled, not crawled exhaustively; the calendar holds 204 published events, which the inventory counts in full.
